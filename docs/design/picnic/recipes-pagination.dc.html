Below the list, 32 px under its last row, as the list’s own row gap, and above the 104 px the dock keeps clear. It’s where someone arrives having looked at every card on the page, so the way on is where their eyes already are (NN/g 6).

Two rows on a phone. “Page 2 of 6” sits centred over Previous and Next, which share the row half and half. In one row, a two-digit page and both named buttons need about 360 px, more than a 390 px phone’s 350 px column; stacked, they fit 280 px at 320 px with nothing scrolling sideways (WCAG 1.4.10). From 420 px of room the three share one row (Tablet and laptop board).

Secondary buttons, as links. Previous and Next go somewhere, so they’re links styled as Dino’s secondary Button (Button asChild), named in words with a chevron beside them, never an icon alone. Neither is peach: the screen’s main action is still “Add a recipe”, and the current tab keeps “where you are”.

No Previous on page 1, no Next on the last. A link can’t be disabled, and a disabled button would need a line under it saying why while “Page 1 of 6” already says it. The other button keeps its own half, so nothing moves under the thumb.

“Page 2 of 6” is plain text, Label type with tabular figures, inside a nav named “Pages”. One page of results, or none, and the whole pager goes.

Which recipes these are, at the top. The count’s line above the list (States board) names the range and the total: “25–48 of 137 recipes” in Small Muted, so someone knows where they are before scrolling, and a screen reader hears it after each page. The pager at the foot keeps “Page 2 of 6”, the unit its links move by. One page of matches keeps today’s “4 recipes”.

Unchanged: the cards, their hearts, the two-a-row grid and its eager first row, which is now each page’s first row.
